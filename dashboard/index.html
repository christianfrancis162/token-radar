How the model works

The model is one function, impliedValue, in lib/model.ts. It fits itself to the snapshot on every run — no multiple is assumed. The page recomputes the same number from the same figures in impliedValueOf, so the number on the board and the number in the detail view are the same arithmetic.

The fitted coefficients

Out-of-sample validation

  • TVL is the size of the business a token would capitalise. The multiple is fitted, not assumed: the 128 protocols that record both a TVL and a market cap are the only evidence of what a token is worth against a TVL.
  • Category moves the multiple. Liquid staking trades at a far lower multiple than DEXs or RWA — the fit reads that off the comparables, it is not a judgement call.
  • Age and chains are small, fitted effects. A longer run with no token and more chains each shift the multiple by the amount the data shows, never by a round number.
  • Seven-day growth is a capped momentum term, not a regression slope. The comparables all come from a down week (their median change is −9.3%), so the fitted growth slope is not significant and would let one noisy week crush fast growers. Growth instead moves the value by at most ±15%, and a fast grower is always worth more.

Read it honestly

  • “Records no token” means the snapshot's record carries no token — it is not proof the protocol has none.
  • A protocol below the watchlist's $50M floor is no worse a subject than one on it: the watchlist is a floor, never the whole board.
  • Implied value is this entry's own claim, never a prediction of a launch price.
  • The validation errors are large because crypto multiples are extremely dispersed — the comparables' own mcap/TVL runs from 0.03 to 1.00. The fit sets the level and the category differences; it does not price a launch to the dollar.
  • Farm steps are a plan, not a promise: airdrops are never guaranteed, and no step here ever asks you to pay to claim.
